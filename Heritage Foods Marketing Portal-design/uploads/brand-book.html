The Marketing Studio

The identity system for a marketing operating system — the mark, the rules that keep it consistent, and the reasoning behind both.

themarketing-studio.com · Prepared for Team Accredian

01

The mark

Three diamond planes standing between the T and the S. The outer two are drawn as outlines; the centre is solid orange. That single contrast is what turns three shapes into one object with a focal point.

02

The idea

An operating system is a stack. Things sit on top of other things, and one layer is the one you are working in. That is the whole concept, and it is carried by the mark rather than explained by the tagline.

The planes are drawn on a fixed 11:24 ratio, so every edge in the identity runs at the same 24.6°. Standing upright and overlapped, the three of them produce a zigzag silhouette that suggests an M without anyone having to force one — you read TM·S without the middle character being a letter at all.

The orange centre is the live layer: the thing currently running. It is the only saturated colour in the system, and it never moves.

Why the outlines matter. An earlier version filled all three planes with tints. It read as three competing shapes. Drawing the outer two lets the eye land in the middle and turns the pair into a frame — the mark becomes one unit instead of a row.

04

Clear space and minimum size

Clear space on all four sides equals the width of one diamond. Nothing enters this zone — no type, no rules, no image edges, no other logos.

Minimum sizes

The outlines are the limit. Below roughly 64px the outer diamond strokes start to close up and the mark greys out. That is the point to switch to the glyph on its own, not to thicken the strokes.

06

Misuse

Each of these breaks something structural, not merely stylistic.

×

Do not recolour the centre

The orange diamond is the one fixed point in the mark.

×

Do not stretch

Scale proportionally. Always.

×

Do not fill the outer diamonds

The outlines are what give the mark its focal point.

×

Do not rotate

The diamonds stand upright. The mark sits on the baseline.

×

Do not add effects

No shadows, bevels, glows or gradients.

×

Do not crowd it

Respect the clear space on every side.

Before launch

Run a trademark search in every jurisdiction you operate in, plus a reverse image search on the mark. Worth a short conversation with an IP lawyer about how copyright and trademark apply to this artwork — they are different questions with different answers.
