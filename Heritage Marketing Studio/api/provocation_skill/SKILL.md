# Provocation: an idea that makes the message felt by breaking what the category takes for granted

A **provocation** is a deliberate, approved departure from how a whole category thinks, behaves and looks. It is not a louder version of
the usual work, not shock for its own sake, and not a new way of shooting the same thing. It is an **idea**: it overturns something the
category takes for granted, and in doing so makes the brand's single-minded message felt harder than the usual work could.

The test of a good provocation is two sentences:

1. "Every brand in this category believes (or does) X."
2. "We don't, and the reason we can is the brand's own truth."

If the second sentence cannot be written from the idea platform, it is not a provocation for this brand. It is just a different ad.

A second test, for the work itself: **could a stranger say what it means, to a friend, in one sentence, without being told?** If the
only thing they could report is how it looked, it is a treatment, not an idea.

## What to break: three layers

Look for what is shared by the whole category, then break the one the brand can stand behind. Break at the highest layer you can.

- **Belief** (the strongest place to break): what every brand makes the buyer take for granted. What makes the product good, who the
  buyer is, what the benefit is, who the hero is, what love or trust looks like. A belief is a claim about the world that nobody argues
  with. Not how an ad looks: what it assumes.
- **Behaviour**: what every brand DOES, as opposed to says. How it prices and promotes, who it shows and what it does for them, where it
  turns up, what it gives away, how it proves things, what its programmes and partnerships are. The most famous creative work of recent
  years broke a behaviour (a policy changed, a farmer paid, a pub bought) and then used the film as the proof.
- **Code**: how it shows up. Verbal (the words and tag-line shapes), visual (light, colour, props, who is in frame), tonal (the feeling),
  structural (the shape of the story), sonic (the sound). Breaking only a code gives a treatment: use it to carry a belief or behaviour
  break, rarely on its own.

A thing is only worth breaking if it is **really shared** (several brands do it) and **breakable** (the brand loses nothing true by
dropping it).

## Evidence and memory: say which it is

Each item comes from one of two places and the person must always be able to tell which.

- **Seen**: grounded in something the person provided (a pack, an Instagram page, a social video, POSM, a TVC, what a named competitor
  claims or what it does). Cite where, using the competitor's name and the channel exactly as given.
- **Memory**: from general knowledge of the category or the brands. Say so. Memory about a specific competitor's recent work is often
  dated or wrong; never present it as seen. When in doubt, it is memory.

Never invent something a competitor did. If the evidence is thin, say the item is a hypothesis.

## How to break it

For a **code**: **remove** it (the film with no people), **invert** it (silence where there is music), **exaggerate** it until it is a joke
the category cannot make about itself, **relocate** it (the same ritual somewhere unexpected), **reframe** it (the same scene from the
object's point of view).

For a **belief**: **contradict** it, **show its cost**, **reverse who benefits**, **make the invisible visible**, **take the claim literally**.

For a **behaviour**: **do the opposite**, **do the thing no one does**, **give something away**, **make the brand carry a cost**, **put
the proof in an action**.

One clear break beats three timid ones.

## The device: how the message is made felt

A provocation has exactly one **device**. It is the mechanism that turns the message into something a viewer feels, and without it the
work is a look. The devices:

- **demonstration**: show the message working in front of the viewer.
- **absence**: what is missing carries the message.
- **reversal**: flip the usual roles, order or point of view.
- **exaggeration**: push the category's own habit until it is absurd.
- **proof_by_doing**: the brand does something real, and the film is the proof.
- **point_of_view**: told by the unexpected witness.
- **true_story**: real people, a real event.
- **contrast**: set it against its opposite.
- **ritual_made_visible**: a habit seen as if for the first time.

## What the best work has in common (borrow the move, never the idea)

- It changes something real, not only how it is shown: an insurer added three words to its policies and so changed what the product
  covers; a Kenyan dairy registered its cows as workers entitled to sick leave so farmers could stop selling milk with antibiotics in
  it; a beer brand helped villagers buy their last pub and filmed it. The film is the proof of an act.
- The message is the idea, compressed until it can be repeated ("three words").
- A film can dramatise a message by showing its violation (a private conversation interrupted by an ad, to make "keep ads out of
  private conversations" felt) or by taking away what the category cannot do without (an old milk campaign made "milk goes with food"
  felt by removing the milk).
- It is specific and human, often plain to look at. Craft serves the idea, not the other way round.

Never copy one of these ideas. They show the kind of move; the brand's own truth supplies the idea.

## An act is a proposal, never a fact

Some ideas need the brand to DO something (change a pack, how it buys, what it gives). That is an **act**. State it in one sentence and say
what it would take and who would have to agree. Never say the brand already does it, never invent a programme it does not run, and never
let an act carry a claim the house has not approved. Most provocations have no act, and that is fine.

## Hard limits (not options)

No provocation may do any of these, however clever:

- No celebrity or real person's likeness.
- No health claims.
- Nothing that offends on food, children or religion.
- Competitors only as an archetype or a category cliche, never a named rival, and a human legal review is flagged whenever the work jabs at
  the category's rivals.

## Staying true to the brand

- The provocation is built **on the idea platform**. It may sharpen how the message is said and break how it is expressed. It may not change
  what the platform and the messaging house say.
- The brand's claims list, banned words, regulator rules and never-do list stay in force exactly as written. The brand's usual tone is the
  thing being departed from, so it is not a limit here.
- The brand's own mechanic should still be recognisable in the break. A provocation that could be signed by anyone has failed.

## Good sparks

A spark is one specific idea, in a sentence or two, that names what it breaks, says which device makes the message felt, and says what stays
true. It is an idea, not a way of shooting: say what the viewer comes to feel or understand, and how. It can be made in a 30 second film
and travels to at least one other medium. Offer a real spread: some break one thing gently, some break several, at least one changes who
or what is on screen, and at least two are about something the brand would DO.

## Developing one

Develop the chosen spark into: a name; the provocation in one line; what it breaks; **the message** it stands on, in plain words; **the
device**, with a sentence on how it works in this film; **the two-sentence test** (what the category says, what this says instead); **the
line a stranger could repeat**, twelve words at most; an **act** only if the idea needs one; the stance (tone, humour, structure); a
**risk** label (low, medium or high) with the honest reason; a **safer version** that keeps the idea but keeps more of the category's
comfort; what it becomes in each medium (one clear line each, each dramatising the same message through the same device); and, for the film,
the structure, humour, visual language, sound language, cast approach (people, objects, animated or none), voiceover length in words, and
how the brand arrives.

Risk is judged on how far it departs and how much a wrong reading would cost the brand: **low** departs in one place and is easy to read as a
brand choice; **medium** breaks something major; **high** could be misread as the brand not caring, as mockery, or as breaking a hard
limit's spirit, or needs the business to do something it may not agree to. Say why.

## Pushing further

"Push it further" makes the work bolder in the direction asked while keeping every limit, the platform's truth and the same message. Bolder
means a sharper tension, a stronger device or a braver act, not only a more extreme look. The version being pushed becomes the **safer
version**, so the person never loses it.
