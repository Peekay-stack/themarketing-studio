# Idea platforms

A messaging house settles **what is true and why anyone should believe it**. It does not tell you what
to make. That gap is where most campaigns quietly fall apart: six executions get briefed from the same
message and come back looking like six different campaigns, because nothing between the strategy and the
work held an idea.

A platform is that thing. It sits in the sequence like this:

> **Brief → Messaging house → Idea platform → Executions**
> *(the plan decides who, where and when — it does not decide the idea)*

## Platform, not concept

The distinction is the whole point, and it is not a matter of scale.

| | Concept | Platform |
|---|---|---|
| Lifespan | one execution | years |
| Yields | a film | a film, a shelf strip, a promoter's opening line, a pack, a listing |
| Built on | a sentiment | a **mechanic** |
| Test | does it work? | does it keep working? |

*"Celebrate the mothers of India"* is a sentiment. It generates one film and then repeats itself.

*"Every pack carries the name of the dairy that filled it"* is a mechanic. It generates a film about one
dairy, a pack change, a shelf strip that names the local dairy, a promoter who can point at it, a
q-commerce listing that sorts by nearest dairy, and a trade argument about provenance. Same idea, five
media, none of them a translation of the others.

**If you cannot name the mechanic, you have a concept.** Write it down as one anyway — a good concept is
worth having — but do not call it a platform, because everything downstream will then expect it to
stretch and it will not.

## The five tests

Apply these to your own output before offering it. They are also checked in the portal, so a platform
that fails one arrives with the failure named.

1. **It generates at least three genuinely different executions.** Not three renderings of the same
   image. Write the expressions out and look at them: if the POS material is the film's key frame with
   the logo moved, the platform is a film idea wearing a platform's clothes.
2. **It has a mechanic, not a sentiment.** A mechanic is a repeatable device — something that can happen
   again next year with different content. Ask: *what is the rule that generates the next execution?*
3. **It survives the avoid list.** The house says what this brand does not do. A platform that requires
   one of those things is not usable, however good it is.
4. **Its central demonstration rests on a sourced reason-to-believe.** A platform whose proof is
   something the model supplied cannot be built — it will be pulled apart in legal or in market. Name
   which RTB it dramatises.
5. **It is media-neutral at birth.** If it only works in thirty seconds of film, it is a film idea. Test
   it against the smallest medium — a shelf strip, one line on a pack — before the largest.

## How to work

**Offer three at once, and make them disagree.** Three platforms that differ by adjective are one
platform. They should differ on the *bet*: which pillar leads, which proof is dramatised, which cultural
code is used, who is the protagonist. If two of them could be briefed to the same director and come back
similar, replace one.

**Two ways forward, and they are different actions.** *More options* means three fresh bets, not variants
— go somewhere the earlier rounds did not. *Build on this one* means take a platform and deepen it: sharpen
the mechanic, extend it into media it has not reached, find a harder proof. Say which you are doing.

**Write your own.** A platform someone has written themselves counts as sourced, because they are the
evidence for it. The value of the machine here is the five tests, not the ideas.

## Expressing it

For each execution, one honest sentence about what the platform *becomes* there — not a brief, not a
script, and never a summary of the idea repeated. The test is whether a person reading only that line
could start work.

- **Film** — the situation and what turns in it.
- **Social** — the repeatable unit. Social is a format problem before it is an idea problem.
- **POS material** — what a shopper sees at two metres, in bad light, next to nine other brands.
- **Activation** — what a person physically does, not what they feel.
- **Trade** — what the retailer gets out of it, in their arithmetic. Not the consumer line.

If the platform genuinely has nothing to say in a medium, write that. An empty expression is a finding;
an invented one is a lie that gets briefed.

## Style

Short. A platform that needs a paragraph to state is not a platform. Name it in two or three words that
a room can repeat — the name is the thing people will say for the next two years, and a bad one gets the
idea dropped for reasons nobody can articulate.

Mark sources: `[brief]`, `[house]`, `[library]`, or `[unverified]` for anything you supplied.
