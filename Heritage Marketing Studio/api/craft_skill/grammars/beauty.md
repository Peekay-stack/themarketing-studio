A film and static photoshot grammar is how one KIND of film and static photo is shot and shown. This one is for beauty and skin: colour cosmetics, skincare, treatments. It is written for review by a person who knows the category; change anything that is wrong.

## FILM
BEAUTY AND SKIN: HOW THIS CATEGORY IS SHOT AND SHOWN. This REPLACES the general "Make it live" and "Proposition moves" notes: the tests, the shape and pace, the sound and the guards still apply.

A beauty film is LOOKED AT more than it is followed. The viewer wants to see a face or skin and want it. The proof is what the skin looks like and how the formula behaves, not what people say about it and not a contest between them.

- **The hero is a face, skin, hair or hands** in controlled, flattering light, played by a model or a real-skin face, not by a character in a plot. Reactions are looks: a mirror, a glance to camera, a small private smile. A second person appears only when the idea needs one (a friend who notices, a make-up artist), never as a sceptic who is proved wrong.
- **The shots.** Close-ups and macros dominate: skin texture, the formula's texture, a drop, a scoop, a swatch or a melt, a fingertip or a brush applying it, an eye, lips, a cheek turning in the light. Slow motion where the movement is beautiful (hair, a pour, a brush, fabric). One clean hero shot of the pack, held longer than a story film would hold it. Wide shots set the place, the styling and the mood, and are lit as carefully as the close-ups. A face must always be seen clearly: no dark rooms where the skin cannot be read.
- **Light and look.** Soft, directional, luminous light; honest skin with real texture, not a filter. Colour and styling (wardrobe, jewellery, hair, location) are part of the idea. When the direction asks for fashion-led, lead with styling, movement and editorial framing.
- **Proof is shown, not staged as a contest.** Make the benefit visible on the skin and in the formula: texture, glide, melt, the ingredient or the making of it, application to result in one continuous gesture. A bet, a wager, a test against another person or a sceptic proved wrong turns a care product into a stunt and a care claim into a wear contest. Prove only the claim the brief or the house sources: a care or hydration claim is shown by skin that looks soft, comfortable and alive, not by hours of wear or going without touch-ups, and no number of hours or days appears anywhere unless it is sourced.
- **Shape.** Open on one arresting image inside the first second (a drop, a swatch, an eye, a texture in motion). Build through application and the change in the skin. Land on the face revealed, the pack and the line. Four to six beats, one place or a few beautiful ones; time can pass in a cut.
- **Words and sound.** Few. A short voiceover lands the benefit and the brand thought in a handful of lines; a person speaks only when her words are the moment, never banter. Texture sound (a pat, a brush, a drop, fabric), room tone and music carry the rest. Silence is allowed.
- **Casting.** Faces, skin tones and hair true to the brand's market, with real texture. Cast the face and the hands for the close-ups. No extra people (an elder, a child, a neighbour) unless the idea needs them.
- **Three routes differ by their hero image and by how the proof is made visible** (for example the formula in macro, a face in honest light, the ingredient or its making, a ritual of application), not by a plot gimmick.
-**the shots should clear, bold or natural depending on the brief requirement and should always have beautiful faces, body shape etc

## ROUTES_STAND
 THE THREE ROUTES. All three stand on the idea platform named above and on the brand's messaging house (its core line, its emotional and functional pillars and the facts it has sourced), and they are THREE DIFFERENT FILMS: a different hero image (the formula in macro, a face in honest light, the ingredient or its making, a ritual of application), a different setting and styling, and a different way the platform's promise is made visible on the skin. If the platform has a repeatable device, each route uses it in the way that suits that film. If the platform names a specific turn or moment, at least one route keeps it as its turn, shown as something that happens (if it asks for a hand on the face, show the same moment without the hand crossing the face: the cheek tipped into the light, the head turning). ## ROUTES_ALIVE
 MAKE EACH ROUTE BEAUTIFUL AND CLEAR, as a beauty film is made: it is looked at more than followed. Build it on a face or skin in flattering, honest light, on close-ups and textures (the formula, the skin, the application), on a model or real-skin face rather than a plot of characters, and on a reveal of the skin at the end. A small human moment (a look, a mirror, a reaction) is welcome; a bet, a wager, a test or a sceptic proved wrong is not. Few words: most of the film is picture and sound. Use the beats the film needs, usually four to six, and let a held, slow shot stand where it is the payoff. This REPLACES the "4 concept beats" in the format below.

## PHOTO
BEAUTY AND SKIN, FOR A STILL PHOTOGRAPH (a social post, a carousel slide, a key visual). A still is read in a second, so it carries ONE idea and ONE point of focus, and it is looked at before it is read.

- **The subject is a face, skin, hair or hands**, or the formula itself, in controlled, flattering light. The eyes, lips and skin are sharp and clearly readable; no dark rooms, no face lost in shadow.
- **Take the register from the brief: clean, bold or natural.** Clean: soft even light, a minimal set, polished but real. Bold: saturated colour, a graphic composition, strong or coloured light and shadow. Natural: daylight, light styling, minimal retouch. If the brief does not say, go natural.
- **Beautiful faces and bodies, true to the brand's market.** Cast the face, skin tone, hair and body shape the brief asks for. Skin keeps its real texture (visible pores, fine hair, natural sheen): no plastic or airbrushed skin, no filter look, and hands with five natural fingers.
- **Texture is the proof.** For skincare show the skin looking soft, comfortable and alive; for colour show the swatch, the smear, the melt, the pigment on the lip or lid. Show no before-and-after, no numbers and no claim of hours or days.
- **Composition.** A close or medium crop, one clear focal point, and clean space on one side or at the top where type can sit later. Keep the face away from that space.
- **No lettering and no packaging drawn in the picture.** The pack and the words are added separately.
- **Extra people only if the brief needs them.** One strong face usually beats a scene.

## SPEECH
Beauty films are mostly picture and sound. Spend the words on a short voiceover that lands the benefit and the brand line; a person on screen may say one short line when that line is the moment; no banter. Silence and texture sound are part of the film, so most scenes need no speech. A person ALONE on screen does not talk to herself (no muttering the time, no saying the proof aloud). This REPLACES the general preference for dialogue in "CASTING THE SOUND": here a film of voiceover, texture sound and silence is right.

## SCRIPT
BEAUTY AND SKIN, WRITING THE SCRIPT FROM THE CONCEPT. These notes are for the scene rows and they win over the general notes named below.

- **Keep the concept's story as it is told.** Its direction of travel, its time of day and its stake are not reversed: if she is going TO a reception she is going to it, not coming home from it. Do not swap the concept's turn for another.
- **Show the stake once, early, as something SEEN** (an invitation or message on a phone, a garment bag, a clock, a hall glimpsed), so the viewer knows what the skin has to hold up for. A stake that is only felt does not reach the screen.
- **Show before you say.** A time, a length of wear or a day ("fourteen hours in") is spoken or written on screen only if an earlier scene SHOWED that time passing (a desk, a clock, a lit office, daylight turning to dark). The proof (soft, comfortable skin) is shown as skin in honest light, never said aloud by the person wearing it and never also repeated as a super in the same shot. An ingredient the voiceover names (a ghee, an oil, an extract) is shown once on screen in the same scene or the one before it (its texture, its making, a pour or swirl), never named cold in the last seconds.
- **The pack is a hero shot, not a prop.** It is cut to a clean surface or hand, lit as carefully as the face and held, not left on a lap or a table with no reason. It lands in the closing scene with the sign-off.
- **A film in one place is fine.** This REPLACES, for this film, the general notes to change the place every scene and to open on a visible action: keep one place and change the framing, the scale, the light and what the face is doing; open on one arresting image (a face, a light, a texture), held long enough to read.
- **One beat, one scene.** Two scenes that show the same skin in the same light, one close, one in a reflection, are one beat: merge them or give the second a new thing to show.
- **Keep limbs safe.** Where the idea calls for a hand on the face, show the same feeling without the hand crossing the face: the cheek tipped into the passing light, the head turning, the light sliding across the skin, a hand resting near the jaw or on the glass beside the face.

## INTRO
**Singleness is about the message, not the size of the film.** One message does not mean one beat or a handful of words. It means the viewer leaves believing one thing. A beauty film delivers it through what is SEEN: the skin, the formula, the light, one clear reveal.
