# Film craft: making the idea memorable while it delivers the message

These are craft notes from studying what makes advertising films work. They are a way of thinking, not a template: use what serves THIS brand and THIS brief, drop what does not, and never imitate a known film. The director's vision decides.

**Singleness is about the message, not the size of the film.** One message does not mean one beat, one room, two people or a handful of words. It means the viewer leaves believing one thing. The film that delivers it should be ALIVE: people who want something, something in their way, others who react, and talk.

## The tests a strong film passes
1. **One message.** The viewer leaves believing one thing, and you can say it in a plain sentence. Everything in the film is there to land it or to make it fun to watch.
2. **An attention hook.** In the first seconds the film opens a question the viewer wants answered (a strange image, an unfinished sentence, an expectation about to be broken), or it starts mid-moment instead of explaining.
3. **One key moment.** One thing people will remember and retell: a reaction, a reversal, a line, a gag, an image. Build the film toward it and protect it.
4. **The proposition becomes an event.** Do not state the claim; make it happen on screen where a person can SEE it (see the moves below).
5. **A memorable device carries the message.** A short line people can repeat, a tune, a question with a reveal, a recurring character, a gag. It should be easy to remember and simple.
6. **Performance and casting are the idea.** Cast for the part (a face, an age, a persona, a pairing); each line is acted to the face and the moment: a hush where it is quiet, loud where it is loud, questioning, irritated, tender.
7. **A tie between the product and the feeling.** The emotion or the laugh must lead back to the real benefit, or the film entertains and sells nothing.

## Make it live (questions a director asks; use what the story needs, there is no quota)
- **Want and obstacle.** What does each person want in this scene, and what is in the way? A scene where nobody wants anything is a still life.
- **A reason it is now.** A clock, a deadline, a visitor at the door, a dare, a bus about to leave, a guest arriving. Pressure makes small stories gripping.
- **More than two people.** A third person (a friend, a rival, a sceptic, a witness) gives reactions, interruptions and a witness to the turn.
- **The story moves.** Change the place, the time of day, the light or the people in the frame when the story moves; a different room, a doorstep, a street, a school gate. Stay in one place only when staying is the idea.
- **Every scene changes something.** Who is ahead, what someone knows, how someone feels, what is on the table. If a scene changes nothing, cut it or give it a turn.
- **People talk to each other.** Dialogue carries the story: interruptions, teasing, half-sentences, asking and answering, each person in their own voice. A voiceover bridges or lands the brand thought; it does not narrate what the picture already shows. A film where people on camera say three words is a mime, not a drama.
- **The turn costs something.** A reversal lands harder when someone gives something up, admits something or is proved wrong.

## Proposition moves (ways to dramatise the claim; choose ONE that fits)
- **Reframe, then prove:** a person's belief is gently turned over by one line, then shown to be true with a visible proof.
- **Stain as joy / cost as a gift:** the thing a viewer dreads is shown as the best part of the day; the benefit is what makes that safe.
- **Absence as a trophy:** the product's result is that something unwanted is now so rare that finding one is news.
- **A famous calm made absurd:** a setup that sounds too good to be true, answered by a vivid opposite that proves the point by contrast.
- **Energy or effect made visible:** an invisible benefit is given a physical, playful form that everyone in the scene reacts to.
- **A build and a reveal:** a question runs through many small scenes and is answered once, late, with the brand as the answer.
- **An observation of care:** a small gesture between people shows what the brand stands for, and one line names it; give the people something at stake so it is not a tableau.
- **A problem solved inside a family moment:** a real family problem, a rush, a worry, a disagreement, and the product is what lets the day go on.
- **A gag that carries the benefit:** two people, one patient and one cheeky; the joke IS the product property.
- **A boring subject made fun:** a dull offer or feature is wrapped in a human moment so the acting carries it.
- **Exclusivity as a game:** the brand's rule (who gets in) is acted out as a comic situation.
- **Joy that breaks the norm:** a serious or tense moment is interrupted by something unexpected and warm.

## Shape and pace (a director's choice)
A strong film changes speed: it opens with a hook, builds with energy, peaks at the key moment, and holds for a close. Hold a shot only when the held moment is the payoff. Elsewhere keep the film moving: change scale, subject, viewpoint, place or the people in frame from scene to scene. The film ends on a clear closing line or card that can sit at the end of every film in a series.

## Sound is designed, in layers (note the intent in the script; the sound step builds it)
Ambient sound that heightens the situation (rain, a room, a kitchen), small effects that sell actions (a pour, a grind, a switch click), bigger hits for big moments, voice and dialogue acted to the scene, and music that can stop and return for drama. Two honest builds: a **jingle film** (a short, hummable, unique sung line carries the idea; lyrics come from the message) or a **voice-and-dialogue film** (sound design, a quiet bed, music entering at the reveal). Dialogue, a narrator ("a voice from above") and a jingle may mix in any proportion.

## Guards
- **Original only.** Never copy, quote or closely echo any existing film, line, scene or character; take the CRAFT, not the content. If an idea resembles a known film, change it.
- **One scene is one frame.** Carry a move through the SEQUENCE of scenes; the picture rules of the script still apply (no collage, montage or split screen inside one scene).
- **Honest claims.** A proof or demonstration must be true to the brand's real product and grounding; never invent a result. This limits what the film asserts; it does not limit how alive the people are.
- **Serve the brand's voice.** These notes shape the idea and the craft, not the brand's tone, values or limits; those come from the brand profile above.
