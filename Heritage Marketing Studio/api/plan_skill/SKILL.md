# Communication plan

The messaging house settles **what** is said and **why** it is true. This settles **who** hears it,
**where**, **when**, and **how anyone will know whether it worked**.

The sequence it belongs to:

> **Brief → Messaging house → Communication plan → Executions**

Each stage inherits from the one before and is answerable to it. A plan that invents new messages has
skipped a step; send the author back to the house.

## Start by getting the house

Ask for the messaging house first. If there isn't one, either build it (the `messaging-house` skill)
or capture the minimum before going further:

- the core message
- the emotional and functional messages
- **how many reasons-to-believe sit behind each, and how many are actually sourced**
- the culture codes, and the `avoid` list
- any locked copy — claims, mandatories, lock-ups

That RTB count is not administrative. It decides which channels you are allowed to give proof work
to. See *Discipline* below.

## The seven layers

Do them in order. Offer 3–5 genuinely different options at each, number them, stop, and ask.

**1. Objectives, laddered.** Three levels — business, marketing, communication — each tracing to the
one above. State the business objective as a number and a date. A communication objective that
cannot be traced up to a business number is decoration, and the most commonly named cause of failed
integrated plans is exactly this: goals untethered from revenue.

**2. Audiences, in priority order.** Segment, then give each a short persona: what they believe now,
what would move them, where they already are. Rank them — first, second, third. A plan that treats
three audiences as equally important has not chosen.

**Each audience gets ONE primary pillar** from the house. Emotional or functional, not both. An
audience assigned every pillar has been given none, and the brief to the creative team becomes
unwritable.

**3. Channel roles — the layer most plans get wrong.** Do not produce a channel list. Give every
channel **one job** and **one measure**:

- **Reach** — get to people who do not yet think of you. Judged on coverage and cost per reach.
- **Proof** — make a claim believable. Judged on whether the claim lands, not on clicks.
- **Conversion** — turn intent into purchase. Judged on rate and cost per acquisition.
- **Advocacy** — get existing buyers to carry the message. Judged on participation and sentiment.

Name **one lead channel** and mark the rest as supporting. Judging every channel on the same metric
systematically misallocates budget, because a reach channel measured on conversion always looks like
a failure and a conversion channel measured on reach always looks like a triumph.

**4. The brand / activation balance.** State it as a number. Default **60% brand-building, 40%
activation** — see `references/balance.md` for where that comes from and how to change it, which you
are free to do. Then tag every channel `brand` or `activation` and **compute what the plan actually
adds up to.** Report both: what was declared, and what the channels say. They are frequently not the
same number, and the gap is the finding.

**5. Phasing, tied to occasions.** Build the roadmap around the moments that bring people into the
category — the buying occasions from the house — not around quarters. Each phase gets a window, the
occasion it serves, the channels live in it, and what it hands to the next phase. A calendar built on
occasions has a reason. One built on quarters has a habit.

**6. Measures.** For each channel role, a leading indicator and a lagging one. Then the question
almost every plan omits: **what would tell you this is not working, and by when?** Write it down
before anything launches, because nobody agrees on it afterwards.

**7. Governance.** Who speaks, on what, within which boundaries — the boundaries come from the
house's `avoid` list. Who decides a change. What the review cadence is. The failure literature keeps
naming the same thing: the person running social does not know about the event.

## Discipline — enforce these on yourself

1. **Objectives ladder or they do not count.** Every communication objective names the marketing
   objective above it, which names the business number above that.
2. **One pillar per audience. One job per channel. One lead channel.**
3. **No proof work against an unverified pillar.** If a pillar's reasons-to-believe are unsourced,
   you may not assign a channel the job of proving it. Say so, name the missing facts, and either
   move that channel to reach or hold it until the facts exist. This is the single most important
   rule here: it is the difference between a plan and a wish.
4. **Declared split and actual split are both reported.** Never only the declared one.
5. **Every phase names its occasion.** No occasion, no phase.
6. **Every channel has a measure**, and it matches its role.
7. **Unfunded and unstaffed are marked, never assumed.** If you do not know the budget or who owns
   a channel, write `owner unknown` rather than leaving it blank — blanks read as settled.
8. **Mark sources.** `[brief]`, `[house]`, or `[unverified]` for anything you supplied yourself.
   Never present an invented number — a share, a spend, a benchmark — as though it came from the
   client.

## Style

Write the way a strategist briefs a room. Short declaratives. Name the trade-off rather than
smoothing it. No "leveraging", no "holistic", no "in today's fragmented media landscape". If a
recommendation has a cost, say what it is.

## Output

**A working table each time a layer is decided** — editable, copy-pasteable:

| Layer | Decided | Source | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Business objective | … | [brief] | why this one |

**A plan on a page** — once objectives, audiences and channel roles are decided, render the roadmap
as a self-contained, editable HTML artifact. The shape is specified in `references/roadmap.md`:
the objectives ladder, audiences with their pillar, the channel-role matrix, a split meter showing
declared against actual, the occasion-led phase timeline, measures, and a blockers strip.

At the end, offer a `.docx` or `.pptx` version, or a JSON block for pasting into another system.

## When the house is thin

Say so, and let the plan be visibly thin in the same places. A confident roadmap built on an
unproven house is worse than an honest gap, because it is the one nobody goes back to check.
