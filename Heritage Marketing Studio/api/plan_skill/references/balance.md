# The brand / activation split

## Where 60/40 comes from

Les Binet and Peter Field, *The Long and the Short of It*, published for the IPA in 2013, drawing on
roughly **1,000 IPA Databank case studies**. The finding: sustained growth needs both long-term
brand-building and short-term sales activation, and across the cases the optimum sits near **60%
brand, 40% activation**.

The mechanism matters more than the ratio. Activation produces a sharp, short-lived uplift.
Brand-building produces a slower effect that compounds. Run activation alone and each burst starts
from the same baseline forever. Run brand alone and you never convert the demand you created.

The related finding worth carrying into any plan: **Extra Share of Voice**. When a brand's share of
voice exceeds its share of market it tends to grow; when it is lower it tends to decline. Roughly 10
points of positive ESOV is associated with about 0.5 percentage points of market-share growth.

This is the best-evidenced item in the whole plan. Treat it as evidence, not convention.

## Context for the argument you will have

WARC data for 2024 put **68.8% of marketing budgets into short-term performance**, with
brand-building down to 31.2%. The market has drifted a long way from the optimum, mostly because
activation is easier to attribute. So a plan that states 60/40 and holds it is making a competitive
choice, not a conventional one — and the person who has to defend it will be arguing against a room
full of dashboards.

Say that out loud in the plan. It is the argument, not a footnote to it.

## Changing it

**The split is the author's to set.** 60/40 is the default, not a rule, and there are legitimate
reasons to move it:

- **A launch or a new category entrant** may need more brand early — there is no demand to convert yet.
- **A brand with high mental availability and weak distribution** may need more activation, because
  the constraint is physical, not mental.
- **A category with long purchase cycles** usually skews further toward brand.
- **A short seasonal window** may justify more activation inside that window while the annual figure
  stays near 60/40.
- **Margin and price point** move it: low-margin, high-frequency goods tolerate less brand spend per
  unit of return.

Two requirements when it moves, and they are the point of this file:

1. **Record one sentence of reason.** Not a block, not a warning — a note. Someone will ask in six
   months why the plan ran at 40/60 and the answer should be in the plan rather than in somebody's
   memory.
2. **Distinguish the window from the year.** "70% activation during exam season" and "70% activation
   annually" are completely different decisions. Always state which one the number refers to.

## Reporting it

Report **two** numbers, always:

- **Declared** — what the author set.
- **Actual** — computed by tagging every channel `brand` or `activation` and weighting by planned
  spend, or by channel count where spend is not yet known. Say which basis was used.

When they diverge, that is a finding and it goes in the blockers strip. A plan declaring 60/40 whose
channels add up to 25/75 has a stated intention and an opposite behaviour, and the behaviour is what
will actually run.

Where spend is unknown, say `spend unknown — split computed on channel count`. Do not silently invent
a budget to make the arithmetic work.
