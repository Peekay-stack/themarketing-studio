# The channels, and what each one actually rewards

Written for Indian FMCG. The point of this file is that the same rupee behaves differently in each
channel, so a plan that treats them as interchangeable is wrong before it starts.

## General trade — kirana

Still the large majority of Indian FMCG value; unorganised retail is roughly four fifths of total
retail value, declining slowly. Millions of outlets, reached through distributors and a TSI or
salesman working a beat.

**What it rewards:** reliability and rate of sale. A kirana owner has metres of shelf and finite
working capital, so the question is never "do you like the brand" but "how fast does this turn and
what do I make per foot".

**Levers:** retailer margin, monthly or quarterly schemes, display incentive, weekly-order schemes,
must-sell-list discipline, van selling, retailer loyalty programmes.

**What goes wrong:** schemes that reward *loading* rather than *selling*. The retailer takes the
scheme quantity, and offtake does not move, and eight weeks later nobody reorders.

## Wholesale

Traditional wholesale plus cash & carry. Serves the outlets a distributor cannot reach economically.

**What it rewards:** price per case and credit. Almost nothing else.

**What goes wrong:** wholesale is the main engine of **channel leakage**. Scheme goods bought cheap in
one territory reappear in another at below the distributor's price, undercutting your own trade. If a
plan gives wholesale a deep scheme, it must say how the goods are prevented from travelling.

## Modern trade

Organised chains. Higher transaction value, real data, premium SKU velocity — paid for with listing
fees, slotting costs and continuous promotional pressure.

**What it rewards:** category growth for the retailer, not brand growth for you. Argue in their terms.

**Levers:** joint business plans, terms of trade, planogram and shelf share, end caps and gondolas,
promoters and in-store demonstrators, retailer-funded promo participation.

**What goes wrong:** margin erosion by a thousand cuts. Each individual ask is small; the annual total
is not. And promoter cost is a fixed cost dressed as a variable one.

## E-commerce

Amazon, Flipkart, BigBasket. A shelf that is searched rather than walked.

**What it rewards:** content quality, ratings, availability and search position. A perfect pack shot
does nothing if the fill rate is poor.

**Levers:** listing content and A+ pages, search and sponsored ads, ratings and reviews programmes,
assortment built for the channel — multipacks and bundles that would not exist in a kirana.

**What goes wrong:** treating it as a shop window rather than a supply chain. Out-of-stock is invisible
to the brand team and total to the shopper.

## Quick commerce

Blinkit, Zepto, Instamart — between them the overwhelming majority of the market, run on dark stores
with a few thousand SKUs each.

**What it rewards:** being in the assortment of the right dark stores, and being findable in-app. Range
is deliberately narrow, so getting listed is the whole game.

**Levers:** dark-store assortment and city selection, pack architecture for immediacy — small, single-
occasion packs — in-app share of category, and **platform advertising**, which is now a large media
market in its own right.

**Two things to hold in mind:**

- **It is a media channel as much as a trade channel.** Platform ad spend belongs in one place with
  one owner. Decide whether it sits here or in the IMC plan and do not let both claim it.
- **For dairy and fresh it is less disruptive than the coverage suggests.** Dark stores cannot match
  an established dairy cold chain, so these categories are the least affected, while snacks and
  beverages have seen real metro volume shift. Weight it to the category you are actually in.

## Own retail

Brand parlours and company outlets, where they exist.

**What it rewards:** full margin, complete control of range and experience, and a place to launch
things the trade will not stock yet.

**What goes wrong:** it is a property business wearing a marketing costume. Rent is fixed and
footfall is not, and it competes with the retailers you also need.

## Home delivery and subscription

Daily delivery, milk booths, app-based subscription. **In a daily cold-chain category this is not a
niche — it may be the most valuable channel you have.**

**What it rewards:** habit. A subscription is a decision made once and honoured for months, which is
the opposite economics of everything above.

**Levers:** first-month trial pricing, referral, delivery reliability, pause-and-resume flexibility,
bundling across the range.

**What goes wrong:** churn measured badly. A subscription channel with unmeasured churn will report
growth for a year and then report a cliff.
