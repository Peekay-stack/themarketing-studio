# How PR work fails

Each of these is a specific, repeatable failure with a tell you can check for in a draft. None of them
is a matter of taste.

## The release that is an advertisement

**Tell:** adjectives in the lead. "India's finest", "revolutionary", "unmatched", "state-of-the-art".
Also: the company as the subject of every sentence.

**Why it fails:** a journalist reads three sentences and files it as marketing. Nothing in it can be
quoted, because a claim with no source behind it is a liability to print.

**Fix:** make the lead a statement of what happened, with a number and a date in it. Move every
adjective into the quote, where it is attributed to a person and therefore reportable as opinion.

## The objective borrowed from the sales target

**Tell:** a PR objective containing revenue, volume, share, conversion or a percentage of anything sold.

**Why it fails:** the programme gets judged on a number nothing it does controls. It will look like a
failure whatever it achieves, and next year the budget goes elsewhere.

**Fix:** the code refuses these outright — a PR objective descends from the plan's `communication` row.
Ask what people should know, believe, or repeat.

## The key message with nothing under it

**Tell:** a message that sounds like a tagline. "Purity you can trust." "Goodness in every drop."

**Why it fails:** the first question a journalist asks is "how do you know that", and there is no answer.
Also, pull-through cannot be measured against something unfalsifiable — every article "carries" it and
none of them do.

**Fix:** every message points at a reason to believe from the house. If it cannot, it is a slogan and
belongs in the film, not the release.

## The 200-name media list

**Tell:** a list with no beats, no recent coverage, no last-verified dates.

**Why it fails:** journalism turns over fast. Beats move, desks close, people leave. A list assembled
once is wrong within months, and a mass email is the single thing journalists complain about most — it
costs the relationship that was the only real asset.

**Fix:** ten to fifty names, each with a beat and a note on what they last wrote that is relevant.
Short and current beats long and dead.

## The English-only launch

**Tell:** a media map whose only tier is national English.

**Why it fails:** for most Indian readers, attitudes are formed in the state-language press, and the
top two titles in each language market hold more than half the readership. An English-only launch
reaches the people who already know the brand and misses the ones the campaign was for.

**Fix:** lead with the language press. It is a short list, which is why the omission is inexcusable
rather than merely expensive.

## The video news release that is the ad

**Tell:** music on the b-roll. Supers burnt into the footage. A cut that resolves to an endframe.

**Why it fails:** a desk cannot put an advertisement inside a bulletin. It needs footage to cut under
its own voice-over. Scored footage is unusable, and nobody rings to tell you — it simply is not used.

**Fix:** clean elements. Silent b-roll, a separate named soundbite, stills, profile, release. The code
refuses a scored master as b-roll.

## The machine-translated claim

**Tell:** a translation with no named checker, or one produced after the English was signed off and
never re-read.

**Why it fails:** a legal line rendered by a machine is not that line in the new language. In a script
the team cannot read back, nobody downstream catches it — the first reader who notices is the regulator
or a competitor.

**Fix:** a named person checks each language, and the check clears whenever the text is edited.

## The paid voice presented as earned

**Tell:** a "credible expert" or "independent commentator" who received a fee, a free product, or is
related to somebody at the company.

**Why it fails:** it is a legal breach, not a grey area. CCPA's 2022 guidelines require disclosure of
any material connection — money, free product, family relationship, equity — with penalties to ₹10 lakh,
₹50 lakh on repeat, and a one-to-three year ban on the endorser.

**Fix:** earned means nothing of value changed hands. Anything else is an endorsement, belongs in the
paid `influencer` medium, and discloses.

## AVE, and the report that impresses nobody twice

**Tell:** coverage priced in rupees. "₹4.2 crore of earned media value."

**Why it fails:** it treats a demolition and a rave as identical, has no meaning for a digital mention
that occupies no column inches, and is rejected by AMEC, IPR, PRSA, PRCA, the Global Alliance and ICCO.
The first time it is quoted it sounds impressive; the second time somebody asks how it is calculated.

**Fix:** pull-through, prominence, and circulation by language, with what is missing named.

## The recall statement written from memory

**Tell:** a draft that asserts what a regulation requires without the obligation having been entered and
confirmed.

**Why it fails:** it is legal advice from something that is not qualified to give it, at the moment of
maximum consequence. And the categories genuinely differ: food has a codified recall procedure with a
public notice; cosmetics does not.

**Fix:** the framework is a reference table a client's legal team confirms, with a source and a review
date. The class comes from QA. The statement is assembled from pre-approved language and signed by a
named person.
