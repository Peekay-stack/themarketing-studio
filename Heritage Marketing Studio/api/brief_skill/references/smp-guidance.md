# Single Minded Proposition (SMP)

The SMP is the single sentence that resolves the tension uncovered by the NeedScope read and the CB/CA → DB/DA shift. It is the strategic spine of the brief and every recommended action must ladder up to it.

## Definition

> A Single Minded Proposition is one sentence, max 14 words, ending in a full stop, that names the one thing the brand is for the consumer that no competitor can credibly claim.

It is not a tagline. It is not a campaign theme. It is the underlying truth from which taglines and themes are written.

## How to construct it

Triangulate three inputs:

1. **The NeedScope position.** Anchor territory + strategic bridge direction.
2. **The CB/CA → DB/DA shift.** Specifically the desired attitude — the sentence the consumer must come to believe.
3. **The competitive whitespace.** What no competitor on the wheel can credibly hold.

Then write a sentence that:

- Compresses 3–4 ideas into one feeling (geography, value, ambition, accessibility, etc.).
- Names a productive tension only the focal brand can resolve.
- Reads like a fact, not a slogan.

### Worked example (Parle-G)

| Input | Value |
|---|---|
| NeedScope position | Anchored in Belonging; bridge into Power via "G for Genius" |
| Desired attitude | "Affordable ambition that respects everyone" |
| Competitive whitespace | Tiger has power but not affordability; Dark Fantasy has indulgence but not affordability; Patanjali has tradition but not forward-facing |

SMP: ***Parle-G is India's most affordable act of ambition.***

That sentence:
- Compresses four ideas: Indian, affordable, active choice, ambition.
- Names a productive tension only Parle-G resolves.
- Cannot be plagiarised by any competitor because each of them fails at least one anchor word.

## The four tests

Every SMP draft must pass all four:

1. **Tension-resolving.** It names a productive contradiction (e.g. affordable + ambition, mass + premium, simple + powerful) only the brand can hold.
2. **Brand-true.** It can be said without abandoning the existing equity. The current consumer recognises themselves in it.
3. **Competitor-defeating.** At least one named direct competitor would have to lie or fundamentally restructure to claim it.
4. **Future-facing.** It allows the brand to grow into new occasions, new variants, new audiences without breaking equity.

If a draft SMP fails even one test, iterate. Three drafts is normal; one-shot SMPs are rare.

## Defending the SMP

Every SMP in a brief must be followed by a "Why only [brand] can say it" defence. This is a table or short paragraph naming each direct competitor and the specific anchor word they fail.

Template:

| Competitor | Why their version of this SMP collapses |
|---|---|
| Competitor A | Claims [word in SMP] but [structural reason they cannot earn it]. |
| Competitor B | Claims [word in SMP] but [structural reason they cannot earn it]. |
| Competitor C | Claims [word in SMP] but [structural reason they cannot earn it]. |

This defence is not optional. If the SMP cannot be defended head-to-head against each direct competitor, it is not single-minded enough — sharpen and retry.

## "What this unlocks" list

After the defence, always provide an illustrative platform-thinking list:

- **Brand line** — one short line that puts the SMP into brand-voice. Tag as illustrative.
- **Mass line** — a vernacular or mass-market interpretation. Tag as illustrative.
- **Activation idea** — a programme or platform that lives the SMP in the world (a scholarship, a ritual, a public commitment).
- **Product or pricing idea** — a tangible product or pricing move that productises the SMP.

The phrase "illustrative — not final copy" must appear next to this list. The brand team owns the final wording; the SMP is what they should never compromise on.

## Common pitfalls

- **Two ideas in one sentence.** "Parle-G is India's most affordable biscuit and the most loved" is two SMPs. Pick one.
- **Adjective stacking.** "Trusted, affordable, ambitious" is not an SMP, it is a wish list. Compress to one feeling.
- **Generic claims.** "We help India grow" could be said by any brand. The SMP must be defensible against the specific competitors on this wheel.
- **Saying what the brand does instead of what it is for the consumer.** "Parle-G makes glucose biscuits" is a product description. "Parle-G is India's most affordable act of ambition" is what it is for the consumer.
- **Naming the category.** "Parle-G is India's most affordable biscuit" locks the brand into the biscuit category forever. "Parle-G is India's most affordable act of ambition" releases it to grow.
