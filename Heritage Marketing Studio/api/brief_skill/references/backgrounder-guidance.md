# Backgrounder

The backgrounder orients a reader in the evidence before any framework mechanics start. A third-party
agency or a non-marketing stakeholder opening this brief should be able to read section 2 alone and
understand what's actually going on in the category and for the brand, before section 5 ever mentions
NeedScope. It sits directly after the Executive Summary.

## Definition

> The backgrounder is the brief's evidence base, written up front: what the category is doing, where the
> brand stands today, what consumers actually think, and the one problem the rest of the brief exists to
> solve.

It is not a second executive summary. The executive summary previews the brief's conclusions; the
backgrounder is the evidence a reader would need to check those conclusions.

## The four parts

| Part | What it captures | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| Category perspective | Category size, growth or decline, structural trends (fragmentation, private-label pressure, channel shift) | Market-share / category spreadsheets, category-level deck content |
| Current situation | The focal brand's own share, penetration, and distribution trend — where it stands today, not the category | Market-share / HH consumption spreadsheets, read verbatim |
| Consumer insights | What consumers actually think, need, and are blocked by — specific and citable, not a restatement of a whole deck | Qualitative research, brand trackers |
| Problem statement | The one paragraph naming the real business/marketing problem | Synthesized from the three parts above, filtered through the brief ask |

## Writing each part

### Category perspective

Two to four sentences. What is the category doing as a whole — growing, flat, fragmenting, consolidating
around a few players? Read from spreadsheet aggregates when supplied (real numbers, not eyeballed), or
from a category-level slide/section in an uploaded deck. If nothing category-level was supplied, say so
rather than reaching for general category memory dressed up as a finding.

### Current situation

Two to four sentences, about the focal brand specifically, not the category. Its share, its penetration,
its distribution — using the real figures verbatim where a spreadsheet was uploaded, the same discipline
the pricing/distribution snapshot already follows. State the trend direction (rising, falling, flat), not
just a snapshot number, when the data supports it.

### Consumer insights

Three to five bullets. Each one a specific, citable finding — a need-state, a perception, a barrier, a
verbatim worth quoting — never a generic restatement like "consumers value quality and trust," which
could be written about any brand in any category without reading a single file. Pull from qualitative
research where it was supplied. If several sources corroborate the same finding, that is worth saying (it
is stronger evidence than one source alone). If nothing qualitative was supplied, say so.

### Problem statement

One paragraph, no more. This is the hardest part to write well because it has two failure modes:

- **Too vague to act on**: "Heritage needs to grow" is not a problem statement, it is a wish.
- **Actually a solution in disguise**: "Heritage needs a new campaign" skips past diagnosis straight to
  a prescription — the brief hasn't earned that yet.

A working problem statement names the specific gap the category perspective, current situation, and
consumer insights together point to. Formula: *despite [what's working], [the brand] faces [the actual
constraint], because [the underlying reason surfaced by the evidence].*

### Worked example (Heritage Foods, dairy — illustrative)

| Input | Value |
|---|---|
| Category perspective | AP/Telangana dairy is a large, largely branded milk category but a fragmented, mostly-unbranded curd category. |
| Current situation | Heritage leads milk penetration and distribution in its home markets (~0.33 and ~0.77 respectively), but its first-choice/consideration rate trails its awareness by a wide margin. |
| Consumer insights | Awareness and trust are high but taken for granted; quality is assumed rather than actively noticed; curd is bought loose or unbranded by habit, not by brand preference. |

Problem statement: *Despite leading distribution and near-universal awareness in its home markets,
Heritage is not the brand consumers actively choose first — trust is assumed rather than demonstrated, so
purchase reverts to habit and price instead of active preference, and curd in particular is ceded to an
unbranded default rather than claimed as a branded occasion.*

Notice what that problem statement does NOT do: it doesn't say "Heritage needs a new tagline" (a
solution) and it doesn't say "Heritage should grow" (a wish). It names a specific, evidenced gap between
what the brand has earned (distribution, awareness) and what it hasn't (active first choice) — which is
exactly the gap CB/CA → DB/DA and the SMP then exist to close.

## How the problem statement hands off to CB/CA

The problem statement is diagnosis; CB/CA → DB/DA is the planning bridge that turns diagnosis into a
communication job (see `references/cb-ca-db-da-framework.md`). They must agree with each other — CA
(Current Attitude) should read as the attitude that produces the problem statement's gap, and DA (Desired
Attitude) should read as the attitude that would close it. If the problem statement names "quality assumed,
not noticed" and CB/CA's shift line is about something unrelated, one of the two is wrong — reconcile them
before moving on to the SMP.

## Common pitfalls

- **Restating the executive summary.** The backgrounder is evidence; the executive summary is conclusions.
  If a sentence could sit in either section unchanged, it belongs in the executive summary and the
  backgrounder needs a more specific replacement.
- **A problem statement that is really a KPI.** "Awareness needs to increase by 10 points" is a target,
  not a problem. Ask what's causing the gap the target would close — that's the actual problem statement.
- **Manufacturing consumer insights with no source.** If no qualitative research was uploaded, say so.
  A confident-sounding insight with nothing behind it is worse than an honest gap, because the reader has
  no way to tell the difference until they try to act on it.
- **Category perspective that is really just the focal brand's numbers again.** Category perspective is
  about the category as a whole — if every sentence names only the focal brand, it belongs in current
  situation instead.
