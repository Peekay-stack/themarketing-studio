Two rules, both from how on-ground actually fails:
1. A CROWD IS NOT A RESULT. Every idea must say how somebody who engaged becomes a number you can act on — a scan, a sign-up, a pack bought there, a name taken. An idea whose outcome is 'awareness' or 'engagement' is one nobody can judge, and you must not write one.
2. UNMANNED IS FURNITURE. Say what the promoter actually does and says. A table nobody is working is a table.

For each idea:
  what   — the central action, in one sentence. What a person physically DOES. Not what they feel, learn or engage with.
  who    — who it is for, and who runs it
  where  — the venue key from the list, and how many sites
  when   — the daypart and the window, and why that one
  how    — the mechanic, step by step, as a field team would run it
  capture— how engagement becomes a measurable number
  access — who has to say yes, from the venue's `access` above
  risk   — the venue's trap, in this idea's terms, and what you do about it
