**Rule:** after any render or storyboard, look at what a viewer sees and judge it in three roles — director (does every scene earn its place, is anything repeated, is the story and the core message clear), cinematographer (does each shot have a job and a different angle, are frames clean), editor (rhythm, length vs importance, flow, where the sound lands). Report problems first; technical correctness is the second paragraph. A contact sheet of one frame per second is the minimum evidence.

**Why:** on 28 Oct I analysed the first coverage film (30.0 s, cuts exactly where planned, ended on the pack) and told the owner it "works as designed". My own sheet showed six drinking shots in a row, which I described as "the same three angles" without asking whether a viewer needs them. The owner's reaction: very boring, shots add nothing, flow not taken care of, bad frames rendered, and "you need to think like a director, cinematographer and editor combined". The extra shots exist for a cinematic feel and must not take away from the story, the entertainment value or the core messaging.

**How to apply:** (1) before saying a film is good, ask: what is each shot for, what would be lost if it were cut, do two neighbours say the same thing, does the pace match the tone (tender = slow), does the last shot carry the message; (2) check the SCRIPT for what the scenes mean (here the logline meant time passing, not angles); (3) check frames for collage/reference bleed, odd limbs, extra people before blaming the engine; (4) design rules so the PRODUCT does this for any film, not only the one in front of me (see [[coverage-and-film-craft-thread]] and the framework doc).
