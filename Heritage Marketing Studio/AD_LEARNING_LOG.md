# Reference-ad learning log

Append-only. One entry per ad, in the order read. Questions, evidence levels and the comparison table are defined in `AD_REFERENCE_FRAMEWORK.md`; read that first. An entry is corrected only by a dated note under it, never rewritten. Started 30 Oct 2026.

## Rules of the log
1. **One ad is a hypothesis; three ads that agree are a pattern; a pattern becomes a proposal; the owner decides.** No rule in the builder changes off one entry.
2. Every statement carries its evidence level (MEASURED, SEEN, READ, INFERRED, OWNER-REPORTED, NOT JUDGED). I cannot hear: sound and music are measured or owner-reported, never described by ear.
3. Views and age are a popularity signal only.
4. The "candidate changes" list records, for each candidate, the ad ids that support it and the ad ids that contradict it, so a change is never argued from the ads that agree alone.
5. Short quotes only (a few words); nothing from an ad is stored or downloaded.

### 001. Cadbury Dairy Milk: "Cadbury Dairy Milk and Cricket are back" (read 30 Oct 2026)

**Fact sheet.** READ: channel CadburyDairyMilkIn (1.62M subscribers), 3M views, "13 years ago" (so about 2013), 26K likes, tag #ShubhAarambh; the description is one line (the T-20 season is about to begin, Cadbury Dairy Milk and cricket are back, "stay tuned for the magic"). MEASURED: 44.34 s, picture 586x480 (a low-resolution, nearly square source). Language: Hindi (sung lines shown as captions; one burned-in end super). INFERRED (from grain, clothes, picture quality, the words "कुछ खास है" on screen): a season-start teaser rebuilt from an older (early-to-mid 1990s looking) cricket film, with a later couple shot at the end. Commercial job: season-start awareness and brand-to-cricket association.
Evidence note: the Hindi lines on screen are YouTube's own auto-captions (white on a dark box, partly garbled, e.g. a line about "steering"), so they are READ at low confidence; the only burned-in words are the end super "असली स्वाद ज़िंदगी का" ("the real taste of life"). The first and last two seconds are black and silent.

**Audience verdict (SEEN, as a viewer).** I lean in at the scoreboard showing 99 (5.2 s) and the extreme close-up of the batsman's eyes; the cuts keep climbing through the bowler's run-up and the crowd looking up. A burst of three cuts in under a second (24.3 to 25.2 s) tips into the release: a woman in a bright floral dress is up on the pitch dancing while a policeman tries to catch her, and the film HOLDS on her for 6.4 s in one wide shot across the outfield with pigeons. I would not swipe: something is always changing, and the wait has a point. The memorable image is the dance on the open grass with the officials and the crowd around. (The story, a batsman near a hundred and a woman who cannot contain herself, is INFERRED from the 99 on the board and the stills; I did not see the ball.)
