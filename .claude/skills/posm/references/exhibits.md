# Reading the exhibits

Thirteen real Indian FMCG pieces, in `references/exhibits/`. Held as internal art-direction reference —
the brands are their owners'. Read for **structure**, never to copy a layout.

The point of this file is that the composite grammar in `key-visual.md` is not a theory. It is what these
thirteen pieces have in common.

## The pieces

### `pantene-sonakshi-broken-cycle.jpg` — person-in-benefit
Portrait, roughly 1:2. Endorser lower-left in three-quarter view, holding a length of her own hair — the
benefit is the hero and she is holding the evidence. Three packs float mid-right, one of them a cutaway
showing the serum. Logo top, full width, largest element on the piece. Headline below it in two weights, the
emphasis carried by bolding two words inside the sentence rather than by a second type size. A separate
support claim mid-right: *"Up to 6 times stronger hair.*" Footnote bottom-left in 6pt.

**Worth stealing:** the eyeline. She looks out of frame past the packs, which pulls the eye across them.
**Note:** six distinct elements, on one piece, and it still reads at distance — because only one is large.

### `dabur-chyawanprash-100-illnesses.jpg` — endorser-with-pack
Square. Headline occupies the top third in heavy gold caps, two lines, the numeral doing the work. Endorser
centre, fist clenched, presenting the pack forward at chest height. Light-streak graphic sweeps behind him.
Deep red-to-orange field. Footnote bottom-left.

**Worth stealing:** the gesture. The pack is held out toward the viewer, not beside him.
**Note:** the claim is huge and its footnote is 2% of its size — legally necessary, visually irrelevant. That
asymmetry is standard practice and it is deliberate.

### `dabur-honey-stay-fit.jpg` — endorser-with-pack, split-field
Portrait, near A3. Diagonal split — white upper, yellow lower — which is doing two jobs: separating the
endorser from the pack lock-up, and giving the type a clean field. Question-and-answer headline structure
(*"WANT TO STAY FIT?" / "START DRINKING."*) with the question inside a weighing-scale device. Pack plus a
glass with a spoon, lower-left. Endorser full-figure right. Base band: tagline, care line, footnote, social.

**Worth stealing:** the diagonal. It creates two type-safe zones out of one frame without a slab.
**Note:** the most element-dense exhibit here, and the only one that could be called crowded.

### `dabur-honey-heart-health.jpg` — metaphor-object
Square. Honeycomb formed into a **heart**, dripping honey down into the pack below it. Foliage background,
warm backlight. Headline lower-left in three type treatments across three lines. Four benefit marks stacked
down the right edge, each an icon plus two or three words. Brand block top-right.

**Worth stealing:** everything. This is the strongest piece in the set. The metaphor is one object, reads as
a silhouette at any distance, needs no talent, and dramatises an invisible benefit that no photograph of a
person could carry. Cheapest to produce and most distinctive.
**Note:** four support marks is the ceiling. A fifth would not read.

### `surf-excel-matic-sanath.jpg` — endorser-with-pack, curved field
Portrait 4:5. Upper half a stadium scene with the endorser arms-folded and a batsman mid-action behind him;
lower half a deep blue field separated by a **curve**, not a straight edge. Pack lower-right at an angle,
with a NEW roundel. Headline lower-left, three lines, mixed italic and bold. Brand device — the splat — set
beside the type as a graphic element rather than as a logo.

**Worth stealing:** the curve as a divider, and the brand device used as punctuation.

### `surf-excel-holi-daag-acche-hain.jpg` — occasion
Portrait 9:16. Two children, arms up, throwing colour; the powder forms a **heart** in the air above them
and the festival greeting sits inside that heart. Pack lower-right. Base band: brand device plus the
tagline, full width, locked.

**Worth stealing:** the occasion carries a *relationship*, not just an event. Two children, not one.
**Note:** the product claim is entirely absent. On a festival piece, relevance is the argument.

### `surf-excel-matic-smart-shots.jpg` — product-in-use
Portrait 9:16. A white shirt suspended inside a violet water vortex, drum texture behind. Headline top,
short, caps. Pack lower-right with a NEW flash.

**Worth stealing:** the mechanism is *visible*. The vortex is the demonstration, not a glow standing in for
one.

### `motherdairy-buffalo-milk.jpg` — pack-led launch
Square. This is the closest exhibit to a pack shot, and the differences from Exhibit A in `failures.md` are
instructive: the pack sits on a **real surface** with a real reflection, there is a filled glass beside it
(the product out of the pack), a bokeh retail background gives depth, a NEW LAUNCH flash sits top-left, a
certification roundel bottom-left, the brand block with its tagline is locked bottom-right, and the headline
is set in three deliberate treatments — *"Presenting Pure"* light, *"Buffalo Milk"* in a large script, then
a support line.

**This is the piece to compare against when the answer really is pack-led.** Even here, the pack is not
alone: surface, glass, background, flash, roundel, brand block, three-part headline.

### `motherdairy-haldi-milk.jpg` — occasion
Portrait 3:4. Upper two-thirds photographic: mother and daughter laughing, outdoor green, a golden swirl
graphic linking them to the product. Lower third a solid orange field carrying the whole type block —
category descriptor, name in script, Hindi line, benefit line — plus the pack, a Tetra Pak, honey, and
turmeric root as ingredient cues. An IMMUNITY BOOSTER stamp top-left. *"Now Available in Tetra Pak."* flash
right.

**Worth stealing:** the hard horizontal split. The photograph is never asked to host type.
**Note:** English and Hindi in one headline block, which is normal and needs a font that covers both.

### `motherdairy-dosti-range.png` — range-array
Portrait 9:16. Five packs arranged as a group — bottle, cone, tub, carton, pouch — with cartoon hands and
feet added so the group reads as friends. Headline top, three lines, ending in a hashtag. Doodle graphics in
the corners. Logo top-right. Footnote base.

**Worth stealing:** the array is given a *reason to be a group* — the limbs make five SKUs into one idea.
Five packs with no device is a catalogue.
**Note:** no single pack leads, which is the acknowledged cost of this type.

### `nivea-men-derma-control.jpg` — person-in-benefit
Square-ish. Model upper-left, arm raised to expose the underarm — the benefit zone is the composition.
Headline right, two weights. Two packs lower-left. NEW flash and ingredient callout right. Three support
lines stacked lower-right, one with an underline device. Logo left, on the light field.

**Note:** the busiest piece here after Dabur Honey's *Stay Fit*, and it works only because the model's arm
creates a large clean diagonal for the type to sit in.

### `nivea-creme-soft-milk.jpg` — person-in-benefit
Portrait 3:4. Model lower-left, towel-wrapped, eyes closed. Product range on a plinth lower-centre with a
rose as a prop. Deep brand-blue field with a subtle wave pattern — the pattern is a brand asset doing the
work a plain gradient would not. Headline upper-right, four lines, emphasis by colour rather than weight.
Four bullet claims lower-right in small caps. Logo top-right. Base band with a call to action.

**Worth stealing:** the patterned field. It is unmistakably this brand even cropped to a fragment with no
logo in it.

### `nandini-kmf-dealer-board.jpg` — dealer board
Landscape, roughly 3:1. Brand name in Kannada and Latin, both large, with the logo device centred between
them. Co-op mark top-left, GSTIN top-right. **The retailer's own details occupy the lower third**: shop
name in Kannada, then MOTHER AGENCY, then door number, street, landmark, city and two phone numbers.

**This is a different problem from every other exhibit.** The board is a template with a variable field, and
the retailer's information is the reason it gets put up. Any dealer-board adaptation must reserve that band
and specify what fills it — see `formats.md`.

## The thing to notice first: almost none of these are photographs

Read casually, these look like photographs with headlines. They are not. **Nine of the thirteen are flat
colour fields with cut-out elements placed on them** — the subject masked out of its background, the pack
masked, both floated on brand colour, with type built in blocks and devices on top.

Count them:

| build | exhibits |
|---|---|
| **Layered graphic** — cut-outs on flat colour, no photographic scene anywhere | `dabur-honey-stay-fit`, `nivea-men-derma-control`, `nivea-creme-soft-milk`, `pantene-sonakshi-broken-cycle`, `motherdairy-dosti-range`, `nandini-kmf-dealer-board`, `dabur-chyawanprash-100-illnesses`, `surf-excel-matic-sanath`, `motherdairy-buffalo-milk` |
| **Photographic** — a real scene is the argument, plus a hard flat band for type | `dabur-honey-heart-health`, `surf-excel-matic-smart-shots`, `surf-excel-holi-daag-acche-hain`, `motherdairy-haldi-milk` |

Even the photographic four never let the photograph host the type. Mother Dairy Haldi is a hard horizontal
split — image above, solid orange below — and every word lives in the orange.

So the diagonals, curves and splits described above are **not dividers inside a photograph.** They are the
edges between flat colour zones, and the cut-outs sit on those zones. Mistaking one for the other is the
error that produces a poster which is technically correct and looks nothing like POS material.

## What all thirteen share

1. **A flat colour field doing most of the work.** Either the whole ground, or the zone that carries type.
2. **Hard-edged cut-outs, shadowed to seat them** on that field — subject and pack as separate layers.
3. **A hero that is not the pack.** Twelve of thirteen. The exception (`motherdairy-buffalo-milk.jpg`) still
   surrounds the pack with surface, glass, background, flash and roundel.
4. **The pack, present and recognisable, in the lower third, at 15–30% of frame height.**
5. **A real brand block**, cornered — every single one.
6. **Type built, not merely set** — multi-weight headlines, shaped badges, highlight boxes, NEW flashes,
   underline rules. Never a slab dropped over a photograph.
7. **A base band** carrying the tagline, then a footnote strip, then often a care or contact box.
8. **A mandatory or footnote line** wherever a claim is made.
9. **One dominant element.** Even the six-element pieces have exactly one thing that is large.

## What none of them do

- Put a full-bleed photograph behind the whole piece and set type on top of it.
- Float the product on a plain gradient with nothing else.
- Put type across the middle of the pack.
- Omit the logo.
- Leave a headline floating alone on a field with no device.
- Make a claim with no footnote.
- Use more than four support items.
